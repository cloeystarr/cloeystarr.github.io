The recut / October 21, 2026

Where Do Memories Live: returning to the cut.

An earlier cut, the original poem, and the next decisions. Film in progress.

Words & work by Chloe Starr

I put the previous cut of Where Do Memories Live? on YouTube. I wanted people to see the work in progress.

It’s my first time finishing anything cut-wise. That matters to me. It also doesn’t mean I think the film is perfect. I keep seeing things I want to recut, and that’s happening across the work. Even The Author, our anime promo for ompom.ai, needs another pass.

The poem is short. The decisions aren’t.

The poem begins with fabric bringing a memory back. Making that into a film asks for choices the words can leave open: which room, which clothes, whose reaction, how long to stay before the next image. My own writing and photographs are the reference material. They still have to become actions on screen.

In the earlier passes, a face could change when an angle changed. Clothes could make separate encounters seem like one evening. A striking frame didn’t guarantee that the next frame would hold the same person. Those problems became part of the recut.

Before generating another shot

The current pre-production pass has 44 editorial shots, with reaction coverage and a shooting order grouped by setup. Blender stand-ins are being used to work out framing, movement and timing. They’re planning tools. They don’t yet establish likeness or performance.

That distinction belongs here, in public. A shot plan is progress. It isn’t a new finished film. The YouTube cut is something you can watch now; the next version is something I’m still building.

The next cut has something to answer to

A public version makes it possible to compare the next idea with a film that already exists. The shot plan, reference work and sequence review have different jobs. One organizes the next production pass; another holds the appearance and relationships I am trying to preserve; the cut shows what a viewer actually experiences.

These are development materials, not a promise that the next export is finished. I want the journal to keep the decisions visible alongside the film, including why I am changing it. The question comes from my poem. The pictures have to earn their place beside those words.
