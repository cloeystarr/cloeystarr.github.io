Who gets to tell the story?

Pixels After Dark became a film about community, machines that act, and becoming the author of my own work.

Words & work by Chloe Starr · Retrospective written October 21, 2026

Pixels After Dark was my favorite event of Tech Week. The film I brought back keeps returning to authorship.

Machines were learning to act. Artists were making work in the room. Those things belonged in the same film because I was thinking about how to become the author of more of my own work.

Keep my perspective in the room

The published film follows conversations about computer agents, literature, retelling and making spaces for people. Simular hosted the event. The voices and contributors have their own credits; my point of view isn’t a substitute for naming them.

The production records show revisions around community, authorship, the narration and the sound. The fact that a talk was recorded didn’t settle the shape of the film. The cut still had to decide why these moments belonged together.

Put the film out, with its context

The publication record shows the film and a five-post thread, published October 16. The thread carried the names, sources and the context that couldn’t all sit inside a short caption.

That is the kind of record I want this journal to make easier to follow. Someone can watch the film, then follow the work around it. A gathering can be the beginning of a piece, instead of a thing I post once and lose in the feed.

A computer can act. What do I want to author?

The production record moves from a historical comparison into the gathering, then through conversations about agents, performance and authorship. That structure took several passes. The narration and sound were revised so the film could hold my perspective alongside the people speaking in the room.

Simular, the host, works on agents that operate computers. Its Agent S repository lets a reader go beyond the event clip and examine the company’s open research framework. That is more useful than treating the demonstration as a self-contained spectacle.

The event also gave me material about making spaces for people and telling stories. Those parts of the film matter beside the technical work. I’m building a body of my own writing and films, and the question of who gets to make and circulate a story is already part of the work.

Thank you to Simular and the people whose recorded contributions appear in the film. The published credit thread preserves the participant names and source distinctions across the longer cut.
