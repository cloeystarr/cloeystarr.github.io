I want my world
to answer back.

Ancient Witch has a world. Now I want her to pick up the phone. A trip through VapiCon, voice agents and the introductions I wish someone could make.

VapiCon · November 11–12, 2026Written November 18 · Published November 19, 202648-second field filmOriginal voices + clean Chloe VOCompanion speaker Shorts below

Film, writing and creative direction: Chloe Starr. Cover: creator collage with an imagined camera and telephone.

Ancient Witch already exists in my creative world. Giving her a voice would make the encounter change with the person who arrives. Someone could ask a question, interrupt or describe a project I had never imagined. I want her to become a superconnector for my network, with the context to suggest why two people might want to talk.

I make films and characters. Usually the person watching cannot interrupt. Voice makes that a much more interesting arrangement: tell Witch what you’re building, throw her a question I never wrote, see what she does with it. At VapiCon, I was looking for the pieces that could make her answer.

Tell her what you’re making.
Let the next question change the route.

I made an ancient witch. Obviously the next sensible step is to give her a phone number.

At VapiCon I went looking for the pieces: a voice, a useful next step, and a way to rehearse the awkward calls.

A little film from my world to yours.

Jordan Dearsley / Vapi co-founder / CEO

Please interrupt the character

A campaign has a script. A caller brings a whole life. Jordan’s event-floor interview describes an ambition for businesses that feel more like people across the channels where customers reach them. My interest is the conversation that follows: someone can interrupt, ask a different question or tell a story the brand never planned for.

Imagine a Harley-Davidson character hearing about the ride you’re planning. A first-time rider and someone who has crossed the country bring different histories. That is my imagined example of a one-to-one brand relationship, not a reported Harley-Davidson deployment.

Witch would start with your project. One odd detail might be the reason she thinks of someone. I want that little leap of recognition, the one that makes an introduction feel personal.

Mehmet Murat Ezbiderli / ServiceTitan / Principal Architect

The call has a job to do.

A burst pipe makes voice AI easy to explain. Someone needs to reach the right person and get help moving. In Vapi’s ServiceTitan customer film, Mehmet describes agents configured for different businesses to take calls, capture leads and make bookings. The voice sits inside a workflow: the conversation needs to produce something the business can act on.

A burst pipe is an unforgiving audience. Nobody needs an enchanting conversation that leaves the water running.

For Witch, the thing that needs to happen is an introduction worth making. Both people still get to say yes.

For Ancient Witch, my version of that job is a useful introduction.

Chloe Starr · November 19, 2026
Bets Margulies / Vapi / Product Manager

Give her a rehearsal

Bets’s walkthrough gives the voice a way to improve after a call goes badly: propose a change, simulate conversations, get a person’s approval and try a limited rollout. Vapi announced Agent Loop in early access on November 11. That assembled improvement loop is the new launch here. The individual problems of testing and monitoring agents already existed.

The agent gets a rehearsal before the next version meets real callers. Its instructions, tools or configuration can change. The underlying language model is not being retrained by this process.

I can already imagine Witch making a dreadful match. Or getting “I’m building something in AI” and needing considerably more to go on. Those are conversations I would like her to rehearse before involving my friends.

Before Ancient Witch starts introducing my friends, I want to rehearse the awkward calls.

The wrong match. The vague project. The moment she should ask before acting.

Olivier Vroom / voice research

Olivier’s grandfather used voice assistants after becoming blind. In Olivier’s study of 137 older adults, formal versus informal style did not reliably change the main outcomes, including trust. A character can have a wonderful voice and still be missing what the person needs from her.

The next experiment

Who should you meet?

I want to bring builders into the film and onto my podcast with a specific scene to try. A person describes a project. Witch asks a follow-up, suggests a connection and explains why. The people involved decide whether an introduction happens.

The live system still needs to be built. For now, I’m making films around the encounter: you tell her something you care about, and somewhere in that strange little world, she thinks of a person.
