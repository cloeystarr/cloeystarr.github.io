Reading / From my archive / October 21, 2026

Before the software, there was the question of attention.

An old paper on Prometheus Rising turns out to contain a conversation I’m still having with my work: what do we notice, and what do we build around it?

Words & work by Chloe Starr

In an old book review, I was already writing about hardware and software. The subject was the mind.

The paper was about Robert Anton Wilson’s Prometheus Rising. Reading it now as archive material, I can see questions that sit beside my current work: attention, explanations, the models we use to make sense of something. That doesn’t make every claim in the paper something I would repeat today.

A quarter on the street

One exercise I described asks the reader to picture finding a quarter, then look for one. In the paper I set two explanations alongside each other. You could interpret finding it as selective attention: you were looking, so you noticed. Or you could decide the mind had made it appear.

Those are different claims. Finding the quarter would not, by itself, establish which explanation was right. What interests me in returning to this passage is the gap between an experience and the story we make from it.

A room, and all the reasons you arrived

Another passage describes sitting in a room and listing the causes that brought you there. In my review, I understood the exercise as a way of following connections: one choice leading to another.

It’s useful to put that earlier writing beside this site. The books aren’t only a collection of titles. Some have left questions in the work, including questions I still need to examine rather than turn into answers.

Keep the source and the interpretation apart

This is an archive reading, drawn from my own paper on Prometheus Rising. It isn’t a new endorsement of Wilson’s eight-circuit model or the paper’s other scientific claims. The part I want to keep working with is more specific: how attention and explanation can change what we think we are seeing.
