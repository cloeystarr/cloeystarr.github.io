Who gets to keep what an agent learns?

At EPAM and AI Circle, an infrastructure question came back to the personal agents I was trying to build.

Words & work by Chloe Starr · Retrospective written October 21, 2026

Does every enterprise need its own agent harness? That was the question I took into the field film from EPAM and AI Circle.

I went with Chris while thinking about the personal agents I was building with ompom.ai. My data was scattered across different places. The question of what sits around a model was close to what I was trying to make for myself.

The layer around the model

The panel brought together Mark Hoffmann from Meta, Aman Mehta from Snowflake AI and Alena Sorokina from Microsoft, moderated by Shahed Serajuddin from EPAM. My film uses original footage, narration and panel sound.

A short event film can’t contain the whole conversation. The edit has to decide which question carries it. In this one, it was the infrastructure around agents and the relationship between that infrastructure and the person using it.

Keep the question attached to the work

The post became a thread so the film could sit with speaker names, credits and sources. The same material later went to YouTube. The music, Reverie by Scott Buckley, is credited under CC BY 4.0.

That movement between places is now part of the pipeline too. The event doesn’t disappear into a camera roll, and the film doesn’t have to lose its context when it leaves the original post.

What survives after the model answers?

A harness is part of what makes a model useful in a particular situation. In my own work, the situation includes reference photographs, writing, previous choices and things I have already asked to change. Repeating all of that at every step is part of the problem I’m trying to address with ompom.ai.

The panel gives that question a broader setting. Mark Hoffmann, Aman Mehta and Alena Sorokina appear in the published credits, with Shahed Serajuddin moderating. Their names and companies belong beside the recordings, rather than disappearing behind a generic headline about agents.

Thank you to EPAM and AI Circle for bringing the conversation together. EPAM’s engineering and product-development work gives the question a practical setting: how does an agent become part of a system people can actually use? My field film carries my own question into that discussion, without claiming that a short recording settles it.
