Portals / San Francisco / November 4, 2026

Valuable
to whom.

The work can keep moving without losing the choices that make it mine.

Chloe Starr · Published November 5, 2026

Elastic{ON} SF. The pitch for the day: your data has never been more valuable. Agreed. So why does the person it's about get the least say in where it lives.

Chloe Starr on X · November 4, 2026

I’m building a creation studio from things I’ve already made. Poems, photographs, films, all the material I want my agent to be able to find and work with. So this isn’t an abstract question for me. It’s my archive.

Why does it keep starting over?

I don’t want to keep explaining the event, the people, the films, what I’m trying to build. I want the agent to carry that understanding forward.

Anish Mathur’s context-engine work gets at something very familiar: an agent can spend its time discovering what the data means, then do the same discovery again on the next question. His approach keeps that understanding available for the next task.

For me, it’s not just finding a file. It’s remembering that this is the version I chose. That this person made the photograph. That a poem can be finished while its film isn’t.

Elastic · Anish Mathur · October 8, 2026
“the context that decided whether an agent succeeded usually wasn’t in the conversation at all.”

From his context-engine article. The source, its relationships and the updates have to stay connected.

Victor’s presentation brings in another part of it: the harness around the model. LangChain’s Deep Agents work combines planning, files, memory and delegation, with human approval at important decisions. The model is only one part of what lets an agent keep working.

That’s much closer to what I want from the Ancient Witch. The point is to keep the work moving, not create another thing to manage.

The archive is already here.

Han Xiao shows photographs from his laptop. Looking at his own work afterward, I found Omni, a local search application that works across pictures, writing, audio and video.

I already have all these kinds of material. What I want is to move between them without losing where they came from.

Let it work. Keep a boundary.

The panel I filmed turns to what the tools can reach and how to limit that access. Kassy Murphy’s published writing takes the question further: the danger isn’t only what an individual tool can do, but how actions can be chained together at a different speed.

THOR Collective · Kassandra Murphy · June 23, 2026
“The cadence is what fails.”

In AI Has Entered the Villa., she uses Love Island to talk about cyber threats, then gets very specific about the gaps between actions disappearing.

I want the work to move faster. I’m asking for that here, too. But I still want to choose what gets published. Researching someone, following them, borrowing their work, and posting a film are different actions.

The useful distinction is between making me supervise every repetitive step and keeping me involved in the decisions that matter.

The photo booth sent me through several decades. Then I went back for the white-jacket version. A Miami crime lord. That one’s going on the fridge.

I want to make things from the things I’ve already made. And I want the agent to remember what I’ve chosen.
