Poetry film / Making-of / October 22, 2026

The photographs were already a world. I wanted to move through it.

Three lines about coffee, my own archive, and the work of turning a photograph into an action.

Words & work by Chloe Starr

the smell of coffee roast
lines my nose
while I wait on this post

This is poem number 14 on my website. It is small enough to pass over quickly. Making a film of it meant spending more time inside it.

I wanted coffee to have a physical presence. Pouring, spilling, smelling, tasting. I wanted to touch the things in these pictures. The fruit, the cotton candy, the doll. Otherwise, I was just looking at photographs while my voice played.

Another artist’s world, with me inside it

The original photographs are by Katy Shayne. Her work gave this film its objects, colors and strange rooms. Those things mattered before any animation happened. I wanted to preserve the feeling of the shoot while finding a way to move through it.

In a 2014 interview, Katy named David Lynch, Sylvia Plath, Wuthering Heights and Thomas Hardy among her influences. Those were connections I wanted to explore in this recut too. That does not mean adding a castle to every picture. For me, it meant making the room feel larger than the frame, letting something ordinary become a little unsettling, and using color to carry emotion.

A hand has to do something

Some of the earlier generations were pretty, but I still wasn’t doing anything. A cup was there. A doll was there. I wanted a performance through the props.

The revised shots gave each object a job. The coffee spills before the hand stops. I lift the fruit. Cotton candy stretches into threads. My hand pulls a curtain, and the next room begins on the other side. The doll’s head turns because I touch it.

The curtain also solved a problem with the two looks. I could disappear behind it, then appear in the flower-crown room. The transition lives in the edit. It doesn’t need to turn my face into somebody else’s.

The poem sets the length

One version kept going long after I had finished speaking. I liked some of the footage, but it had wandered away from coffee. We saved those clips and brought the moving story back around the poem.

I recorded three readings with different inflections. This cut selects from two of them at their original speed. The written poem appears before the rolling credits. I want these to have the form of films, even when the poem is only three lines.

The sound had to become part of the room

The roar in the earlier mixes took me out of the film. My phone recording was material to work with, but I wanted it to feel composed. The new mix keeps my voice and piano separately editable, with the music making room for the words.

The piano is Kevin MacLeod’s recording of Gymnopédie No. 3, after Erik Satie. It is credited under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0. The quieter score leaves space for the pictures without filling every moment with another effect.

What this attempt taught us

The motion was rehearsed locally in Blender before the new Wan generation. The useful improvement was being specific about the action and staying close to the original reference. We kept the rejected takes too, so a later cut can be compared with what actually failed.

Thirteen renders were preserved. The Vast balance fell from $7.62 to $5.89 through generation and the time the stopped disk remained stored. That is a displayed decrease of $1.73, not a final itemized invoice or the total cost of developing the film. The temporary instance has now been deleted.

There are still places where the model shows through. The stilts become a surreal turn toward the house and window, rather than a convincing walk through a doorway. I can see the difference. Of our poetry-film attempts so far, this is the one I like best.

I want to keep finding out what else is in these pictures.
