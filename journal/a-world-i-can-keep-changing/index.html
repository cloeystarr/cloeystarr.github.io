Portals / Building in public / November 5, 2026

A world I can keep changing.

I wanted my website to look like Breathless, but in color. And I wanted it to be able to change with me.

Words & creative direction by Chloe Starr · Built with Codex

I wanted my website to look like Breathless, but in color. It’s my comfort movie. I keep coming back to that question: is it better to have grief or nothing?

With building, I wonder if there’s a version of that question too. Is it better to make something, or never make it at all?

OMPOM had started to feel more like a place I wanted to be. My own website still felt like a bunch of different websites.

I had the films, the poems, the writing. But I couldn’t really walk around my finished artwork. And when that work went onto Instagram or LinkedIn, I wanted it to feel like it came from the same person.

So the goal got bigger than fixing the homepage. I wanted a creative identity that could move across all of it, including the work my agent does for me. OMPOM could be related without having to look exactly the same.

Breathless is my comfort movie. I wanted my website to look like Breathless, but in color. The question I keep coming back to is: is it better to have grief or nothing? With building, I wonder: is it better to make something, or never make it at all?

Chloe Starr on X · November 5, 2026

What would they make now?

Lynch and Noé are in my references too, along with Baudrillard and Robert Anton Wilson. But Breathless is the film I actually put on for comfort. That feels more useful to say than just putting it on a moodboard.

I kept asking what French New Wave filmmakers would make if they had AI agents, generative cinema, and interactive websites. I didn’t mean make my purple portrait black and white. I wanted that willingness to experiment with the tools we actually have.

We looked at Codrops and other creative-web references. But adding more things to click wasn’t making it more cinematic. Some versions had more labels and explanations than work. I kept rejecting them.

The things I liked were pretty specific: my actual photograph, the lowercase name with its orange period, the dark background. The temple was fine. It just didn’t need to compete with me.

And Coffee already had something I wanted to keep. The turquoise, the oversized poem, the original photography. I liked being able to move from a photograph into a moment of the film and come back to the poem.

Someone who met me two weeks ago saw a different website. They might have thought it wasn’t very good. It takes time to make something you like. I’m still building it. I’m changing too.

Chloe Starr on X · November 5, 2026

The work needed room

Watch, Read, Explore sounded simple. Then I clicked around and was more confused than before. Explore what?

I chose Poetry, Events, and Portals. I want people to find the San Francisco films, read an essay, or spend time with a poem. They don’t need every other part of me presented at once.

Human Experience and ENOUGH. are still there. I’m just not promoting everything right now. And a poem is finished writing even if I haven’t finished its film.

Content is liquid

I want to keep making things from things I’ve already made. A photograph gets movement. A poem becomes a film. The film gives me something to write about.

But I want to keep what I chose, too. The original words. My face. The people whose work is in it. Changing the presentation shouldn’t lose the sources or the history.

I want my personal website to be the outside of p(om). Log in, and you’re in your creation studio. Building this in public feels almost like performance art: enacting the startup, and showing why I’m the person to build it.

Chloe Starr on X · November 5, 2026

That’s what I want the website to make easier. The work can look different as it changes, and still feel like mine.

The Ancient Witch is my AI agent. The voice is always mine. She’ll operate across these places for me, including a digital twin. That belongs in this identity too, without turning her into a mascot.

Someone who met me two weeks ago saw a different website. Maybe they thought it wasn’t very good. It takes time to get to something you like.

I think we sometimes see people as stagnant pools. We meet them at one point in time and that’s who they are. But people keep growing and changing. I want room for that here.

Actually, I do know why I’m putting this much effort into my personal website and getting the work out into the world. I want the website to be the outside of p(om). You’d log in and the inside would be your creation studio. That’s the experience I’m working toward.

I’m trying to enact my startup almost as performance art. The films, the writing, the website, where it all goes afterward. This is how I’m exploring what the company could be.

And if I want to raise money, I have to explain why I’m the person to build it. How do I even begin that explanation if I don’t have a personal website?
