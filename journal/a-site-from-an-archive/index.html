Website / Retrospective / October 4, 2026

The archive becomes a place you can visit.

Poems, fiction and photographs. Choosing a public version without replacing the originals.

Words & work by Chloe Starr · Retrospective written October 21, 2026

I wanted a personal website ready for Tech Week. I also had an archive full of material that didn’t fit into a short introduction.

There was poetry, fiction, photographs from travel, old performances and the film we’d been making for ompom.ai. The task was to find a way for someone to enter that work without needing the whole archive.

Choosing a public version

We selected atmospheric photographs for the writing. I wanted images without people, and I wanted more than the first few obvious pictures. The photographs became another way through the page: moving to another line of writing could move to another view.

The fiction needed its own pass. Personal names became old AOL-style screen names, including mine inside the stories. The source documents stayed in the archive. The website could present a selected version without replacing the originals.

Publishing is also selection

The October 4 deployment record is specific about what went up: the site files, 23 selected atmospheric images, and the approved film and poster. The private archive and review files were excluded.

That boundary matters to how I’m building. An archive gives me material to work from. It doesn’t mean every file belongs on a public website. The site starts with choices, then keeps changing as I understand what I want it to hold.

A photograph is also a decision

One of the first choices was atmospheric photography. I wanted images I had actually taken, without people in them, beside the writing. Changing a line could also change the view. That gave the photographs something to do, rather than making them a decorative background.

The site also needed to keep different kinds of work legible. A story could sit in the short story collection. A poem could have its own room. The film needed a player and credits. The home page had to introduce me without explaining the entire archive before someone could enter it.

That is still the work of this site. I can change what it presents without replacing the original files. The archive remains material I can return to, and the public pages become a selected, revisable way through it.
