Process / Retrospective / October 13, 2026

A pipeline is more than a folder of exports.

The first production audit separated technical completion from the thing I actually wanted: a film that worked.

Words & work by Chloe Starr · Retrospective written October 21, 2026

A file could export successfully and still fail to show the thing I’d asked for.

The October 13 audit named a problem that had kept showing up in the work. An image could be related to the narration without performing the action in the scene. A technically valid video could still have a weak transition, an unclear gesture or a face that didn’t hold together.

What changed on screen?

The audit looked at prior film scripts, production records, continuity notes and user feedback. It distinguished those checks from a fresh full viewing of every film. That distinction became part of the method: the record should say what was actually reviewed.

For a narrative beat, the proposed correction was concrete. Record the starting state, the action, the changed state and the person or object that must be recognizable. If the beat is a handoff, a nearby flame image doesn’t establish that the handoff happened.

The sound needed its own attention

Earlier feedback had also called out abrupt music, continuity between images and unprofessional sound. A change to picture could not be the only unit of work. Voices, room tone, effects and music needed to remain separately editable.

None of that established a measured speed improvement. It established a clearer way to describe the work and its defects. That was the useful change: a folder could tell me where an export lived; the pipeline needed to tell me what was wrong and what the next revision was trying to change.
