Design diary / October 20, 2026

A house I can put myself in.

The house, the portrait, and an earlier version of my corner of the internet.

Words & work by Chloe Starr

The company site was live, but my own website still didn’t feel like somewhere I would want to stay.

I had been getting it ready for Tech Week. My writing was there. My films were there. There were photographs from my archive, social links, and more things to find behind an index. Having everything on the page wasn’t making it feel like one place.

Then ompom.ai went live, with the French Riviera-inspired direction I’d been working toward. The house and its linework gave me something to respond to. I wanted the two sites to belong beside each other. I also wanted my personal site to stay white, and to have an image of me.

The house was already part of the story

I’m building ompom.ai around context. My own archive is what I keep returning to when I work: the writing, photographs, performances and unfinished films. A house felt close to what I wanted this personal space to be. In the company’s imagery, there was a house. In mine, I wanted to be inside it. This is my p(om).

That sounds simple until you start making the picture. I liked the portrait, but I wanted the crown, elf ears and outfit to become continuous linework. Those details matter to me. I’d had an elf-themed wedding. I didn’t want an unrelated costume added because it looked vaguely futuristic.

“I want my personal site to stay white and have an image of me … but I am in the house because this is my p(om).”My direction during the redesign

Keep the person. Draw the world around her.

The treatment brings my photographic face and hair into the architectural drawing. The clothing continues the lines of the house. The ears and crown belong to that same language. We revised the crown again because it was still reading as jewelry rather than drawing.

Some of the most useful decisions were things I kept saying no to. I didn’t want the navy background on my personal site. I didn’t want a row of competing fonts. I didn’t want the photograph, poem and video to look as though they’d arrived from three different websites.

A few doors, clearly marked

The navigation needed the same attention. If someone arrived to watch an event film, they shouldn’t have to discover an index and work out where I’d put it. The cover now leads with three links: ompom.ai, events, worlds. The films have a gallery. Poetry has its own room. The short story collection can be a collection of stories.

The sites share typefaces: Bodoni Moda for headings, DM Sans for reading, IBM Plex Mono for small labels, and La Belle Aurore for my name. The work now is carrying that language through the pages behind the cover. A coherent first screen only gets you so far if the next click feels like another site.

This article is part of that work. I want the blog to show the building, with the images and decisions that went into it. Not just announce that a website went live. The site is up. I’m still deciding how to live in it.
