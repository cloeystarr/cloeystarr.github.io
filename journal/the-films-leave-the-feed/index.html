Distribution / Retrospective / October 17, 2026

The films needed somewhere else to live.

Moving the field notes onto YouTube made the archive easier to find. It also made titles, covers and credits part of the work.

Words & work by Chloe Starr · Retrospective written October 21, 2026

I had been posting the field films on X. A growing body of work was starting to look like a disappearing feed.

The October 17 reconciliation records sixteen earlier films on YouTube, alongside the larger field films. Some were Shorts. Some were longer videos. That distinction later mattered when the website mistakenly treated several existing Shorts as films that still needed uploading.

A film has more than a file

Each placement needed a title, description and the sources that had travelled with the X thread. Music licenses and contributor names aren’t something to leave behind because the upload has moved to another platform.

The record includes source-file hashes and verification in YouTube Studio. Those are useful for a practical reason: they help avoid uploading the same cut again under a different filename.

Make the collection visible

The public SF Tech Week playlist brought nineteen field films together. It kept that collection separate from the older acting work and other creative essays already on the channel.

The next connection was the website. A film could have a dated place in Events, play there, and still link back to YouTube and the original X discussion. Distribution was becoming part of the production pipeline: not only making the work, but making it possible to find again.

The small films belong in the collection too

A short arrival clip and a longer researched film need different kinds of room. The first can hold a wrong turn, a person laughing, or a little detail from a gathering. The second can follow a speaker’s work, a research example, and my own response. Both lose something if I can only find them by scrolling back through an account.

The move to YouTube required checking what was already there, including Shorts. A film could be present without appearing in the channel’s long-video view. That mattered later when the website’s own list fell behind the publication records.

The collection now needs a connection in both directions. A person reading an article should be able to play the film. A person browsing Events should be able to follow the making-of story. The original X thread stays available for the conversation and credits around the release.

The collection pictured above is the later website draft, reviewed on October 21. It shows where this distribution work is going: each film has its date, its original sources and a path into the production story. The earlier films remain available through the Events collection and YouTube channel.
