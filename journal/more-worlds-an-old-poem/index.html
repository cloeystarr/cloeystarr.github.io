Field film / Retrospective / October 14, 2026

New world-building tools, an old line of poetry.

The Spatial Intelligence and 3D Demo Day film brought my archive into the conversation about what independent filmmakers might make.

Words & work by Chloe Starr · Retrospective written October 21, 2026

I went to a demonstration of world-building tools and came home thinking about a poem I wrote in 2012.

The line was about my dreams of making films. It had been in my writing long before the tools I was watching at Spatial Intelligence and 3D Demo Day. The film I published on October 14 brings those two moments together.

What can I make from what I already have?

Yi Hua of World Labs, Yanpei Cao of VAST and Tripo, and Jimei Yang of Runway showed different ways of working with captured moments, reference images and existing footage. The event took place during Tech Week on October 8; this entry follows the later film publication.

My archive gave me a different way into the demonstrations. I have photographs, performances, writing and unfinished films. The question was what I could make with that material, and what I wanted to say through it.

A film with several kinds of source

The finished thread kept the contributor credits beside the film. My perspective and footage sit alongside attendee material, official model demonstrations, speaker portraits, OM/POM artwork and a poem animation. Those sources are credited separately.

The result was a field film rather than a product demonstration. The tools are part of it. So is the person trying to find a way to turn years of her own material into work she can author.

Follow the people to their actual work

Yi Hua describes a practice across research, engineering and art. In the film, her introduction leads to World Labs work. Yanpei Cao connects the Tripo discussion to his VAST research and company work. Jimei Yang’s own site gives a longer view of his work in visual synthesis and animation.

The production pass replaced distant, unclear projector shots with properly identified people and official demonstrations. That did not make the demonstrations mine. It made their role in the story easier to understand: these were examples of other teams’ work that I was trying to think with.

Tripo’s P2 preview is a concrete path into the mesh, rigging and animation work. Runway’s GWM-1 research page is the source for its world-model demonstration. The film credits also name attendee contributions from Aiko Dai, Aniket J and Romy Ilano. Thank you to the speakers, organizers and contributors whose work made the piece possible.

Notes

Yi Hua of World Labs, Yanpei Cao of VAST/Tripo, and Jimei Yang of Runway showed tools that change what an independent filmmaker can attempt: moving through captured moments, turning reference images into 3D assets, transforming existing footage.

My archive is full of photographs, performances and unfinished films. I want to turn that material into stories I author myself. That’s why I’m building OM/POM.

This field film brings my perspective together with footage from Aiko Dai, Aniket J, and Romy Ilano, plus the Crayon builder showcase.

Speaker portraits: Yi Hua, Yanpei Cao and Jimei Yang, sourced from their websites. Model demonstrations: World Labs, Tripo and Runway.
