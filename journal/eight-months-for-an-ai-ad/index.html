Eight months
for an AI ad.

An AI company spent eight months making an ad. I went to the premiere at Foreign Cinema.

By Chloe Starr · At Foreign Cinema, November 18, 2026

KERNEL makes infrastructure for AI agents. For its first ad, it spent eight months working with an animation studio in France.

An anti-AI-slop ad for an AI company. Already, I was interested.

Catherine Jue, KERNEL's co-founder and CEO, describes an eight-month collaboration, scenes made by hand and a custom score. The film credits Parallel Studios. The product gives agents access to the internet; the campaign gives that invisible work a carefully made little world.

A person at a desk, a hand at a keyboard, a small character moving through green interfaces. Later, a patient at a reception desk. All these ordinary people trying to get something done, with a rather elaborate little universe working behind them.

KERNEL makes infrastructure for AI agents. Its first ad took eight months with an animation studio in France, with scenes made by hand and a custom score.

An anti-AI-slop ad for an AI company. That was the interesting part of last night's premiere.

Chloe Starr · November 19, 2026

The premiere had a dress code of its own

I wore a yellow jacket with shaggy cream trim, burgundy pants and platform shoes. A healthy amount of outfit. The round green windows at Foreign Cinema obligingly made a frame around it.

Chris and Connor Paton, a co-founder of Tembo, bonded over having their clothes bought for them. Connor's girlfriend shops for him at Abercrombie. I buy a lot of Chris's clothes there too.

My camera went into the kitchen too. Trays of small, carefully arranged bites were being carried out. In the courtyard, the projected greens landed on a red wall and returned in the wet ground. A person could watch the screen, or look down and find another version of it.

Inside, a laughing encounter. Outside, the film was still playing against the wall. I kept moving between the screen and the people who had come to see it.

In my field film, I wanted the commissioned animation to spill into the courtyard. Datamosh lets one image linger as another arrives: a little of that green world gets loose in the party, then the picture clears.

At the KERNEL premiere, I filmed the kitchen and the projection reflected in the wet courtyard.

Now I'm letting the animation leave a little memory inside the footage. Datamosh as an encounter between the film and the room that watched it.

Chloe Starr · November 19, 2026

Eight months is a choice

Eight months, a studio in France and a score of its own. A company building infrastructure for agents had decided this was worth asking people to make.

Catherine’s writing about the agent web keeps coming back to consent: who gets to act, for whom, and with what permission. Specific, traceable, revocable. I like putting that beside the animation. Someone still gets to decide what to ask for, what to keep, when it is finished. I spend a remarkable amount of my own time on the last two.

My photographs here have AI treatments; Parallel Studios made the commissioned animation. Two different ways of making images, meeting in the same courtyard.

And then there’s the last photograph: two faces together, a name tag still on. We went to a premiere. We also had a night out.
