Field films / Building ompom.ai / October 6, 2026

Four rooms, one question about agents.

Permission, evaluation, model comparisons and inference costs. Four short films connect the rooms I visited to the personal-agent work I was building.

Words & work by Chloe Starr · Retrospective written October 21, 2026

The question kept following me through the films: if an agent learns from my work, what can I keep, what can it do, and what happens when something goes wrong?

Four field notes from October 6 approach different parts of that problem. They are short pieces from AI Realized, Post-Training and Agent Infra, Google for Startups with Google DeepMind, and FriendliAI. Their programs overlapped. This is a connection between the recorded films, not a claim that I attended every minute of every session.

Context needs permission as well as memory

The AI Realized panel brought together Adil Ajmal, Jayant Kolhe, Madhav Chinta, Chris Caen and Vasanth Chandra. My film asks who can act on my context and whether that authority can be revoked. A tool remembering something and being allowed to use it are different questions.

Thank you Christina Ellwood and AI Realized for the gathering, K&L Gates for the venue, and JLINC, named as sponsor in the release credits. The film also includes outside work by Algedonic and Vidhi Agrawal, credited as research examples rather than recordings of the panel itself.

A failure needs somewhere to go

The Post-Training and Agent Infra film follows a question about learning that belongs to the user. Peter Pang’s account of a self-healing agent harness gives it an operational example: evaluation has to connect to identifying, repairing and verifying a failure. His article describes his team’s process, not a benchmark I ran.

Thank you Brix, CREAO AI, NeuroSpark AI and Beta University, named in the event credits. For my own work, the useful connection is the loop between noticing a problem and changing what the system does next.

A comparison is a beginning

At The Aviary, the Google for Startups and Google DeepMind film returned to a different test: context, actions, results and recovery. Arena’s own explanation describes blind comparisons and preference voting. That is one form of evaluation. It does not replace asking whether an agent can complete my actual task.

Thank you to Google for Startups and Google DeepMind. Their startup community and research work meet in that room, while the film carries my own building question into the conversation.

Then the workflow meets the inference layer

FriendliAI brought response time and cost into the same picture. Its research article, One Benchmark Is Not Enough to Choose Your Inference Provider, explains why performance depends on the operating conditions and the trade-off between latency and throughput. The figure in my film is a research insert from that article, not a live benchmark at the gathering.

Thank you FriendliAI. The company works on model inference infrastructure. Its research example gives me a more specific question to carry back to ompom.ai: what does the whole workflow need, beyond a good-looking model score?
