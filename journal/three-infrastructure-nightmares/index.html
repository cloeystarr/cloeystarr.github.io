Field notes / Retrospective / October 7, 2026

Three ways the infrastructure can go wrong.

Inference theft, a rollback that didn’t end an outage, and a reminder that AI research is older than its current scaffolding.

Words & work by Chloe Starr · Retrospective written October 21, 2026

The horror-night films needed three parts because they weren’t one story told three times.

Matt Lenhard’s talk concerned inference theft: someone else’s keys and someone else’s bill. J. Paul Reed’s story concerned an outage that survived a rollback. Jennifer Prendki pushed the discussion toward research problems that might be hidden under the plumbing.

What stays with me

Building ompom.ai made the surrounding questions feel close: context, permissions and the harness around the model. Prendki’s talk asks for another level of attention, beyond the latest scaffolding.

The three films use original speaker recordings, with symbolic AI illustrations identified as such. Chris filmed with me and asked the harness question in the Prendki film. The original eerie score and edit were part of the Codex experiment. These details are in the credits because they tell you what kind of material you’re watching.

Further notes, people & sources

Keep the speaker attached to the story

The record includes a correction to speaker attribution. That is part of making the films, too. A compelling edit still needs the right person’s name attached to the right account.

The Reed film describes a crowdsourced incident story, not an incident presented as happening at his current employer. That distinction has to remain when a clip travels. The caption can’t get shorter by becoming less accurate.

Three stories need three sources

The first film follows Matt Lenhard of Vectoral. The second follows J. Paul Reed’s crowdsourced incident story: a rollback does not end the outage, and investigation has to continue across teams. The third returns to Jennifer Prendki’s question about the research beneath the infrastructure.

These are different kinds of failure. Keeping them as three films made space for each voice instead of collapsing them into one warning about AI. The original threads carry the speaker credits and outside sources, and the players below preserve that sequence.

Thank you to Heavybit, SVB and Tailscale, named in the event film credits. Heavybit’s work centers on enterprise-infrastructure companies. Tailscale builds secure connectivity between devices and services. Those are concrete reasons an infrastructure gathering belongs in their communities. The films are my account of the talks, not evidence that any host experienced the incident described.
