Field film / Creative life / October 17, 2026

Purple curtains, a memory room, and a poetry recut.

At Pebblebed’s Mem-Off, an afternoon around AI memory came home with me as editing advice, a little film, and photographs in my purple gingham dress.

Words & work by Chloe Starr · Retrospective written October 21, 2026

I found a room with purple curtains and came home with a few edits for my poetry film. There are photographs of me in a purple gingham dress beside a stack of old televisions. This is also part of building.

Chris and I went to Mem-Off! The Open Source AI Memory Showdown at Pebblebed on October 17. The event put a practical question in front of people working on memory systems: what can an agent actually remember, retrieve and update? My own work gave me a very specific reason to care. I was building ompom.ai and still recutting a film made from my writing.

The useful part can happen beside the program

Alex Newman helped me think through edits for a recut of my poetry film. That was part of my account of the afternoon, and it became part of the short field note. The exchange became part of the field note: a real afternoon connected to the recut I was trying to make.

Alex’s Claude-Mem project is one route into his published work. It is designed to preserve relevant context across agent sessions. His work on persistent context and the editing conversation gave me two different ways into what he was making.

A small film, with the room still in it

The cut uses our original footage and photographs, short written recollections and real room sound. It moves from arrival to the welcome sign, then to people working and the television corner. The photographs and the edit let the afternoon keep its own scale.

Pebblebed describes itself as a home for builders and backs people working on technical problems. Its physical space gave this gathering a setting I wanted to remember. That is why the room appears in the film beside the conversations, rather than being reduced to a location label.

Further notes, people & sources

Memory is a practice, not only a promise

The organizer’s program names Cognee, Claude-Mem, Helix and the wider memory community, with shared scenarios for testing recall and changing facts. The program made room for people to bring their own systems and compare approaches.

Cognee works on connecting knowledge and memory for agents, teams and people. Claude-Mem focuses on carrying useful context across sessions. The event brought those kinds of work into a room where people could compare approaches. Chris had a conversation with Cognee founder Vasilije Markovic, whom I later confirmed by name. The published thanks keep that connection attached to his name.
