Field notes / Security & sound / October 21, 2026

Security, a backpack, and The Chainsmokers.

A security event, a physical-security problem, Cloudflare’s hospitality, and the history between Tiësto and The Chainsmokers. The pieces that make a night, and a film.

Words & work by Chloe Starr

I was at a security event when the group chat started giving me FOMO about Tiësto and The Chainsmokers. Chris and I decided to go to Oracle Park. Then physical security stopped Chris over his backpack. We had left one security event and arrived at another kind.

The backpack stayed outside. His work laptop came to the party with us.

Security, again

At Oracle Park, Chris had to hide his backpack outside, and we carried his work laptop around. I keep coming back to the sequence of it. Security was the subject of the first event. Security became the obstacle at the next one. The laptop became an extra guest.

It belongs in the film as a little interruption, with enough time for the absurdity to register before we get swept into the party.

Taste is an invitation

Cloudflare’s func(tion) closing party took over Oracle Park. I wanted to show the space because the way they put an evening together says something about their taste. There was the little ice cream bar, all the food, somewhere to sit, and then this enormous concert. Those details deserve room in the story.

I think about what it means to host people. Give them something to eat. Give them somewhere to stay for a while. Let the evening have different scales, from a small bowl of toppings to the field and the stage. That’s how I want to look at what Cloudflare curated.

Chris and I were also taking videos of each other eating. We were trying to give each other’s point of view: what he saw of me, what I saw of him. That belongs in the story too. The hospitality became something we were experiencing and filming together.

There are bell peppers in here

And then there were bell peppers growing inside the stadium. I filmed them. Of course I filmed them.

The Garden belongs to Oracle Park; I’m not saying Cloudflare built it. What I loved was encountering it inside this night. The huge stadium, the music, and then these actual vegetables growing. I want the film to pause there.

A song becomes our memory

The Chainsmokers were awesome. I caught pieces of their concert, including “Paris” and “Closer.” I also wanted the “but first, let me take a selfie” sound beat in the film. That cue can be a playful archival quotation alongside the camera turning toward me and Chris, with its source kept clear.

A song has a history before I hear it that night. Then Chris is beside me, we’re in this particular place, and another memory attaches to it. I want the film to have time for that. Let a musical phrase finish. Let a look or a gesture happen. Let us be inside the picture.

A world my dad worked in

I had loved being at Vanta’s AARM Intercept Demo Night. My dad worked in that ecosystem, so the subject had a personal connection for me. Vanta, Insecure Agents, and Keycard were hosting an evening about security for agents acting in real systems.

The AARM specification concerns checking and recording agent actions before they execute. That matters to the world I’m trying to build, where software can act with more of our context. I think security will become even more important as we move toward what I think of as web 4. That’s my interest in the subject, alongside the connection to my dad.

An AI agent can have the right credentials and still do the wrong thing. I came to see how people stop that before it happens. That is the question at the beginning of the film, before another invitation starts pulling at me.

The published program listed demos from Vanta, Keycard, Formal, Ultra, Runlayer, MintMCP, and Browserbase. Their work addresses different parts of the problem. Keycard describes access scoped to the task, rather than treating a working credential as permission for everything it can reach. Ultra describes controls on agent actions and tool content. Runlayer addresses the agents, MCP servers, skills, and plugins already spreading through a company.

The published MintMCP product tour gives us a concrete example to look at: tool activity, flagged commands, and detection rules. That is published product material in the film, not a recording of the stage. Formal’s documented approach narrows controls to commands and queries, while Browserbase provides browser infrastructure and ways to inspect and replay what an agent did. Those are different jobs. Seeing the difference gives me better questions to bring into the room.

Then another room started calling through the group chat. The music, the people, the possibility of joining them. We went.

Someone opened a door for someone else

The Chainsmokers were also telling a story during the concert. The way I remember it, they were talking about Tiësto supporting them early. Hearing that gave the performances another connection. It was exciting that they were both there, and there was a history between them that I wanted to understand.

There is material we can go back to. A November 2014 SiriusXM announcement names Tiësto among The Chainsmokers’ supporters. In 2015, they released “Split (Only U)” together. Spinnin’ Records also published an official clip of Tiësto playing it at Tomorrowland that year.

That gives the film a way to bring the past into tonight: early support, a collaboration, then the people sharing another gathering years later. It doesn’t establish an absolute claim about who played their music first, and I’m not saying “Split” was performed at Oracle Park. The older material has its own date and place. That difference is what makes the meeting interesting.

The artists brought their own account too. The Chainsmokers posted about catching a ride to San Francisco with Tiësto. Their journey and ours ended up in the same room.

My first principles, in the picture

Earlier that day, I had been explaining my filmmaking principles to ChatGPT. For me, this is connected to transcendental meditation and Krishna consciousness: each of us moves through a world of memories, experiences, ideas, and sensations. We encounter someone else’s world. We exchange something and make something together.

The camera participates in that exchange. Where I stand, what I notice, whether I turn it toward myself or somebody else, all become part of the encounter. Editing is where I discover the relationships between those fragments: images, spoken words, ambient sounds, memories, and other people’s material.

That is what I want datamoshing to do in this work. The changes between sources can be visible and playful. They also need a reason. Another person’s camera can give us a view I couldn’t have had. An older performance can bring a history into the present. The encounter should create something that the pieces could not have made alone.

My archive matters here too. More than a decade of artistic references should inform real choices about beauty, framing, duration, and sound. I don’t want the same template put over every experience. I want the work to understand why these particular things belong together.

Other people have part of the night

The film brings in recordings from Jason, Bap, Caedin, Kaushik Immadisetty, and Kylie. Those are distinct contributions, with their names and original posts attached. They give the film more than my position in the crowd.

The point is to find the relationships. The security room and the entry problem. A garden inside the stadium. The artist who supported somebody early, and the people hearing them years later. Me and Chris, there together, carrying a laptop.

It was an awesome night. I want to stay with the people at the end, after all that sound and scale, and let it get a little quiet.
