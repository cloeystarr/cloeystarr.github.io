Founder spotlight · November 13, 2026

He tried to open the money system.
Then the police came.

What Gabriel Jiménez carried out of Venezuela, and what happens when his story meets the people building and using UGLYCASH.

By Chloe Starr · An Ancient Witch Founder Spotlight

Listen to Ancient Witch’s podcast ↓

Gabriel Jiménez signed with Nicolás Maduro on television. The New York Times later reported that armed guards had detained him before that appearance, threatening him with El Helicoide, a political prison.

He had opposed the government. Then he helped develop its cryptocurrency. How did he end up in that room?

The founder behind UGLYCASH had made a bet: perhaps technology could give Venezuelans a freedom their government would never willingly grant. He tried to build it inside the government’s own project.

CHLOE STARR · @cloeystarr

Gabriel Jiménez opposed Maduro, then helped develop his government’s cryptocurrency. He thought technology could open the money system from inside. Before UGLYCASH, there was that bet, the threats, and the decision to leave Venezuela.

Chloe Starr · November 13, 2026

The bet

Before the Petro, there was a payroll. In his Epicenter interview, Jiménez describes the difficulty of paying employees who lacked dollar accounts. Crypto offered a way to get money to people who had already done the work. His ambition grew from that problem into a much larger attempt to loosen Venezuela’s financial controls.

He had returned after time in the United States, hoping to help change his country. Working with Maduro was a choice. The government had power to open a route he could not open alone. It also had power over him.

In March 2018, while publicly defending the Petro, he told Associated Press reporter Joshua Goodman:

“This is about providing oxygen to people, not a government.”

Goodman’s report warned that the government stood to benefit first, and perhaps most. That concern belongs beside the ambition. The project could help the people Jiménez wanted to serve and the rulers he opposed.

The people holding the door

In his 2020 interview with Scott Melker, Jiménez describes a dispute over the Petro’s white paper and its authorship. He says political police came to his home at six in the morning.

Gabriel’s own account · March 31, 2020

Listen to Gabriel’s original interview with Scott Melker, around 50:00 →

The detention around the launch was a separate episode. The investigation by Nathaniel Popper and Ana Vanessa Herrero describes it and the televised signing afterward. A public signature can look like agreement. The room he could not leave belongs in the picture too.

In April 2019, the Times reports, he sold his Toyota Autana to buy a ticket to the United States. A car became a way out of the country.

Leaving did not erase the earlier decision. He had chosen to work on the Petro; later, in his account, he could no longer choose its terms. I want to follow what happened to that question of control when he began building again.

What he makes of it now

UGLYCASH is a later, distinct project. Today its X profile describes an app for “people who make money on the internet.” Creators, freelancers and people working across borders are part of the audience. That brings us back to the original payroll problem: the internet lets you work with someone far away, and getting paid can still be difficult.

GABRIEL JIMÉNEZ · @stablecoinapp · MARCH 19, 2026

“everyone around the world has the same level of access.”

Gabriel’s statement of ambition for UGLYCASH

The company has built a public world around that promise. Its manifesto film moves through blocked opportunities, migration and the difficulty of receiving money from an employer in another country. Its short film about Gabriel gives us a more personal self-portrait: an ambitious man describing vulnerability, mistakes and purpose. In that film, he says, “I’m driven by humanity.”

Those are large claims to make for an app. I want to hear what happens when the founder’s language meets the people making it and the people using it.

The world between the app and the street

In a recorded design conversation, Gabriel sits with Carlo Santi and Tristen Lerma. He talks about artistic work coming through a person’s “surroundings,” “context” and “experiences.” Santi then shows how objects, stickers and street photography move into the brand and interface. Lerma talks about finding a visual language the internet already speaks.

CHLOE STARR · @cloeystarr

In a UGLYCASH design talk, Gabriel Jiménez says our surroundings and experiences shape what we make. I want to put that beside his history in Venezuela, then look at the choices he makes now. What does that experience change about the product?

That changes how I look at the table I photographed. The patches and cameras are part of a conversation the team is already having about how the physical world enters a financial product.

On X, Gabriel had already asked for a physical magazine made from things saved online to be brought into the UGLYCASH space. That movement between a screen and a room shows up in his own feed, too.

GABRIEL JIMÉNEZ · @stablecoinapp · AUGUST 19, 2026

“We need one of those at our @UGLYCASH space”

Gabriel on bringing Offprint into the space

There is a more interesting answer to the design talk on X. Punkable, @0xPunki, a design engineer and creator, describes asking for the company’s poster files during a live broadcast. He then took the posters to a moai and Estadio La Portada in La Serena, Chile, before putting them on his wall.

SOURCE X POST · @0xPunki · August 10, 2026

“Uglycash on my wallet, Uglycash on my wall.”

Gabriel replied, “this is sooooo awesome”. Someone else had taken the work out of the studio and made another small world with it. That is the exchange I want to see: a creator asking for something, the company handing it over, and the result coming back changed.

Punkable’s post includes a referral link. It is an enthusiastic, commercially linked creator account, not independent verification of product claims.

From a manifesto to ten minutes

The next voice makes the scale smaller. In a company-published portrait, Luisa Bueso introduces herself as a virtual assistant working from home. She describes moving money and getting an errand down from three hours to ten minutes. The English captions read: “For me, time is extremely valuable.”

Her story gives that ambition an everyday scale. Getting time back in the middle of a working day. Having more room for the life around the work. That is something I can get excited about.

CHLOE STARR · @cloeystarr

UGLYCASH’s manifesto talks about global financial access. In a company-published portrait, remote worker Luisa Bueso talks about getting an errand down from three hours to ten minutes. I want those two scales in the same conversation.

Chloe Starr · November 13, 2026

The invitation

We popped into UGLYCASH’s new space on November 11 and 12, including after a trip to the Casper store. Ava Gigantino had invited us to their Better Money Dinner Series.

Her Instagram calls her a vibe curator. Her own introduction to the job adds fundraising, strategic initiatives and operations. She writes that “most opportunities don’t come from a perfect plan,” then describes conversations and introductions. Her invitation to us is one small, actual instance of that work.

AVA GIGANTINO · @avagigantino · OCTOBER 29, 2026
UGLYCASH Studios phase 1 ✔️ · Ava Gigantino

Gabriel’s journey brought him here. Other people are helping decide what this place becomes: the designers, the people who film it, the creators who carry its images away, the workers whose money moves through it, and Ava inviting people in.

After everything it took to get here, he has kept building. Now there is a team, creators taking the work into their own cities, and people like Ava bringing us into the space. I’m rooting for them. I’m excited to see what UGLYCASH builds next.
