What happens beyond the interface?

A Gray Area field film followed AI into characters, bodies, worlds and simulated life.

Words & work by Chloe Starr · Retrospective written October 21, 2026

The Gray Area film begins with a wider frame for AI: characters, bodies, worlds, portals, identity and simulated life. A software interface is one way to encounter a system. These examples ask what happens when the encounter takes another form.

An artwork and a product demo are different encounters

Gray Area brings art and technology into exhibitions, education and creative work. The installation named in my film, Dreaming Babies: illi by Yoon Chung Han, belongs to that artistic setting. It should not disappear into a generic montage of AI tools.

New Art City offers a virtual exhibition toolkit, another concrete way to think about a space someone can enter. The film also credits demonstrations from Runway, Google DeepMind and World Labs. These sources make different proposals about interaction and worlds. Putting them together is an editorial choice, not evidence that they use the same technology.

Our own ompom.ai animation appears as our work, with Chris Starr AI credited. That is where this field note returns to what I’m making: stories, images and a website someone can visit. The outside examples open questions around that work, rather than standing in for something I built.

Thank you to Gray Area and the credited artists and teams. The original release links the sources and distinguishes recorded installation footage from outside demonstrations.
