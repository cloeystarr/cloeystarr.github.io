Field notes / Retrospective / October 6, 2026

Books, a brunch, and the shape of a life.

At Claude Founder House, the conversation moved between books, community and the places people might build together.

Words & work by Chloe Starr · Retrospective written October 21, 2026

A brunch became a film about books, community and what survives when a life becomes context.

The women-in-startups gathering at Claude Founder House gave me more than an event recap. I kept coming back to the books in the conversation and the thought of making places with other people.

The books are part of the scene

The published film and thread name Dear Data, Grid Systems in Graphic Design, The Question Concerning Technology, The Art of Japanese Joinery and The Human Condition. They’re different works, with different concerns. Their presence in the film is a record of that conversation, not a claim that they all say the same thing.

The film combines the gathering with generated interludes. That distinction belongs beside the picture. Original event footage, outside artwork and an imagined transition don’t have the same provenance.

The room continues beyond the event

One part of my notes turns toward real estate and community: a place to live, make things and stay up talking about what we’re actually trying to build. That is a more particular memory than a list of people who attended.

The resulting film runs just over three minutes. Its X thread holds the wider reading discussion and credits. The event footage was made with Chris. Filaments by Scott Buckley is credited under CC BY 4.0; the additional atmospheric bridge is separately identified.

The room and the research

The books in the film are not interchangeable symbols for being thoughtful. They concern data, design, technology, making and public life. Putting their titles beside the discussion gives someone a way to follow a particular line of curiosity after the gathering is over.

The event research also leads to Catherine Olsson’s work on in-context learning and induction heads. That is a technical research project. It is not an explanation of personal memory or a scientific justification for the way I think about a life becoming context. Those subjects can sit beside each other without becoming the same claim.

Thank you to Beth Robertson, Catherine Olsson and the Anthropic Founder House team. The program made room for founders to cowork, talk and meet. Brooke Lacey’s attendee photographs offer another account of the brunch. My film uses its own recorded material and keeps the outside contributions credited.
