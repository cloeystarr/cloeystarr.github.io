Portals / November 2, 2026

Content can be
liquid, too.

I went out for Halloween and came home wishing I’d filmed more. So I used AI to reconstruct and extend the night.

Words & work by Chloe Starr

Chris and I walked the runway, stopped at P(DOOM), and caught a little street music. I came home with photographs and bits of footage.

We were Will and Elfaria from Wistoria: Wand and Sword. We had the costumes. I wanted to see us in their world.

I had a photograph. Then I had an imagined photograph of Chris and me. I liked it. I wanted to see us walking together in that world. Then I wanted him to spin me.

The original photographs mattered. I wanted us to look like us, in the costumes we actually wore. Chris is taller than me. My sandals had ankle straps and block heels. My leg coverings had a gold pattern and came down to a point at the ankle.

I kept correcting things. The shoes. The gold. A strand of hair growing out of my jewel. A bracelet I didn’t want there. I wanted to keep the face I liked while fixing the rest.

Being able to change everything isn’t much help if I can’t keep the things I’ve chosen.

At the runway, someone was an OpenAI engineer and someone else was a honeypot. I love that there’s a room where that works as a costume.

These are the people I wanted to go out and see. AI is work, and something we talk about, and apparently something we can dress up as. The joke depends on knowing what someone means before they explain it. I don’t want to lose that part of living here when I make the city look enchanted.

My little piece of footage couldn’t hold the whole P(DOOM) party. In @muse_inmotion’s video, I could see it through someone else’s camera.

Then Mission Delirium’s Punkie Night. The next night, a little DanceOWeen, and someone walking around as a Klombo. So hard to find in Fortnite. Apparently easier to find walking around San Francisco.

The animation gave Chris and me a little more of the runway together. It sits beside the real coverage as a piece of the night I imagined.

I couldn’t go back and film Halloween again. But I could keep making something from it.

Next year, though, I’m getting a much warmer costume.

Runway production notes

I wanted a fuller twirl. The hands and the lines soften a little during the turn. But I liked enough of it to use this take and keep going.

For the runway animation, we composed a beginning and an ending image. Wan received those two images and a description of the action. The character references helped us construct and check the images; they weren’t separate identity controls inside the video model.

The five-second process clip has no verified YouTube upload yet.

Related reading: Patrick Collison on liquid software

Patrick Collison describes software that you can keep changing by asking for what you want. He calls it “liquid software.” I recognize something in that from making this film: wanting another possibility without losing the choices I’ve already made.
