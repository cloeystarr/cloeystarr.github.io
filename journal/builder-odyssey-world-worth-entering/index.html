Field notes / Retrospective / October 18, 2026

I brought a poetry film to a conversation about AI video.

At Builder Odyssey, the panel and the film I was trying to finish became part of the same edit. A 3 minute, 38 second field film from The Midway.

Words & work by Chloe Starr · Retrospective written October 21, 2026

I went to Builder Odyssey with Where Do Memories Live? still in progress. I came back with a field film that keeps cutting between the panel and the work I was making.

The gathering was at The Midway on October 18, hosted by SEAMATE and Linkloud. Chris Starr AI and I filmed there. The finished piece, A World Worth Entering, begins with us arriving, then moves into AI video and short drama. My poetry film enters the sequence while that conversation is still happening.

The film I was making was already in the room

Jonathan Fischoff’s discussion leads into a demonstration, then the edit returns to the panel. A few shots later, my own poem-film appears. That is the connection I wanted this piece to hold: I was listening to people discuss AI video while trying to make decisions about an actual film.

The poem begins with fabric bringing someone back. Turning that into pictures means choosing a room, a person, clothes, a reaction, and a moment to cut. The panel could talk about the possibilities of video. My own work gave me something specific to put beside those possibilities.

Who am I making this for?

Near the end, the edit puts that question on screen. It is my editorial question, not a quotation from the panel. The next sequence returns to Jonathan speaking about a particular community. Then the film comes back to my own work.

That order matters. The film does not end by claiming the tools have solved the problem. It ends with the poem-film, ompom.ai, and enough work in front of me to keep going. Publishing the cut gave the day a shape I could watch and return to.

The soundtrack is Reverie by Scott Buckley, excerpted and mixed under CC BY 4.0. The panel recordings and my voice are our original recordings. The production record identifies the poetry-film images as AI-assisted reconstruction from my writing and archive.

The people who made the gathering possible

Linkloud supports Asian founders and entrepreneurs in SaaS and AI with international expansion, founder meetups, growth and marketing workshops, acceleration programs and consulting. LiberNovo makes dynamic ergonomic chairs, bringing a physical-workspace product into a room full of people who spend long hours building. Further notes, people & sources

Follow the work behind the microphone

Jonathan’s own writing gives me a more specific way to think about motion. In Motion Control with Attention Scaling, he experiments with changing temporal attention to vary movement. He also shows the limits: stronger motion can bring artifacts. This is an earlier AnimateDiff and SVD experiment, separate from fal’s current products.

His Making Videos Lose More asks why judging individual frames can miss what goes wrong between them. The training experiments had stability problems and did not establish a fix. That unresolved part belongs in the story. It connects to a practical problem in my own recut: a frame can look right while the sequence still fails to hold together.

There are other useful paths into his work: his music-directed video experiment on X and the LinkedIn account of that experiment. fal’s H3 Max Director demonstration is a separate example of steering generated video while it plays. It is company work, not something I’m attributing to one person.

Zizhao Yu’s own professional profile names his writing and editorial work, including My Safe Haven in a Frozen World. It gives a project name behind the short-drama discussion, without making unverified audience or revenue claims. Nicolas Zylberstein’s profile connects the distribution conversation to his NewsBreak work. NDot AI’s own account describes helping publishers build direct audiences and revenue. That makes distribution part of the creative question: how does a particular piece reach the people it was made for?

The outside work in my edit can be followed too. Blendi’s UNREEL repository documents an AI video-streaming project. It is third-party work, not a panelist’s project. The film’s source notes identify which demonstrations belong to which makers.

A demonstration and a field recording have different sources

Zizhao Yu and Nicolas Zylberstein appear in the cut too. The sequence moves through short drama, distribution and the relationship with an audience. It brings their recorded voices together with my narration and selected outside work.

The production timeline names those outside demonstrations individually: fal’s H3 Max Director material, ALIBI by Christos Antono, and UNREEL by BlendiByl. Those are their makers’ work. They sit beside our event footage and my poetry-film reconstruction, with separate credits. Showing what I’m learning includes making those differences clear.
